# An Outcome cannot count its own review rounds

The harness requires a ticket's `## Outcome` to be written before the review stage gate, because the reviewed-tree fingerprint covers the whole ticket file and anything added afterwards makes `verify-delivery` refuse. So the Outcome is written before the review that decides whether another round is needed. Any statement in it about how many rounds the ticket took is therefore a claim about the future at the moment it is written: it can only be true if the review it is about to face passes. A ticket that needs one more round has falsified its own Outcome by the act of being reviewed, and the sentence that says so most precisely is the one that goes stale first.

The grammatical form does not matter, and the forms are not a list to check against. A count is stale after the next round. An ordinal makes the reader reconstruct a number the journal already assigns, and is easy to get wrong in a way nothing flags. A comparison or a superlative is worse than either, because it can be false on the day it is written as well as stale afterwards. A distributive claim, that each finding came from its own round, carries no number at all and fails the same way. That last form is the reason this ADR does not offer its list as a test: a claim with no number in it passes any search for counts, ordinals, comparatives and superlatives, and a search can only ever contain the forms someone has already met.

## Consequences

An Outcome states what was built, the evidence for it by record number, and what moved to another ticket and why. It does not count, order, compare or characterise the ticket's own attempts, returns, review rounds or records. Where a reader wants those, the Outcome points at `docs/harness/history/<ticket>/`, which is the only place they are correct by construction, because it is the thing they are counts of.

Two kinds of number remain legitimate and should not be confused with the above. Figures about the work are stable and belong in an Outcome: `slice_points` from the solution records, the criteria delivered, the mechanisms built. Figures about a past, closed act are also stable, such as a count of sentences a named finding corrected. The test is whether the review now beginning could make the sentence false, and it is the only test. If it could, the sentence is about the process rather than the work, and the journal owns it.

An amendment is where this defect breeds, because an amendment moves what the summaries summarise. When a criterion moves, a status changes or a scope narrows, the authoritative statements get updated and the summaries are what get left behind. After any amendment, re-audit every summary and cross-reference mechanically rather than by reading, and treat a clean mechanical pass as weak evidence rather than proof, for the reason above.

The reviewer cannot be relied on to catch these cheaply. Each round costs a return, a regression run against the corrected tree, a fresh triage and a fresh review, and a round can also produce a finding that does not hold, which costs the same. Writing an Outcome that cannot go stale is cheaper than finding out round after round that it did.

The provenance of this record is a single harness ticket whose Outcome was returned repeatedly for this one class of defect while its code stayed green throughout, including twice inside the fixes for earlier instances and once inside the first draft of this ADR. What that history was exactly is in that ticket's journal, and deliberately not restated here: a case study of a ticket's review rounds, written inside the repository that ticket is still being reviewed in, is the very claim this record exists to forbid.
