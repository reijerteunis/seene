# Seen: MVP development plan

Eight two-week sprints from 28 September 2026 to 29 January 2027 and the gate each one has to pass. Tickets per sprint are listed in [docs/tickets/README.md](tickets/README.md).

## Shape of the plan

Eight two-week sprints from Monday 28 September 2026 to Friday 29 January 2027, preceded by four harness days from Thursday 24 September, with a two-week holiday buffer over Christmas. Day 120 of the MVP falls on Tuesday 26 January 2027. The plan is sequenced so that every sprint ends with something a customer touches: the first three sprints build only what the free audit and the recovery loop need, because those are what the POC gate on 30 November measures; the modules come after, one per sprint, in the order a customer would switch them on. The bake-off from the decision tab still stands: offers out by Friday 25 September, decision rule on 31 October. Sprint 0 and Sprint 1 are the "build the free artefact semi-manually on real data" step from that tab, done properly: the audit engine is product code from the first day because the audit is the sales process.

## Team and capacity

The harness comes first. Forty-one tickets (95 build points) build the Seen harness (epic E10) from 24 September, four days before Sprint 0 opens; 24 of them are delivered (56 points), the first twenty on 23 and 24 September and the rest in the first week of use, and the 17 open ones (39 points) all sit in Sprint 0 by the founder's decision of 28 September, because the plan is ahead of its calendar: SEEN-008, the first product ticket, is already in review. Sprint 0 therefore carries 132 build points on a 16-day calendar, 39 of them harness; what the harness does not finish in Sprint 0 rolls into Sprint 1 under the SEEN-123 cap. Ticket size: no new ticket is above 3 points, and every five-point ticket in Sprints 0 to 2 carries a slice plan (slices of at most 2 points, at most four per ticket) that the solution stage adopts or amends; a plan that needs more slices is a ticket to split. The harness is described in `docs/harness/workflow.md`.

Local first, cloud by decision. Development and the first pilots run on a local Docker environment (SEEN-097: Supabase CLI stack, Redis, Mailpit, telemetry collector, `pnpm dev:up`), and CI runs the same services. The code talks to a secrets provider, a storage provider and telemetry that switch to Secret Manager, the Supabase EU project and Cloud Logging by configuration. Google Cloud is provisioned only when Ruud records a go decision; the go-live ticket (SEEN-007) sits in Sprint 2 because that is when pilot brands start approving actions in the inbox, and until then a Cloudflare Tunnel from the local environment is the interim, with pilot data on the development machine and the DPA saying so.

Correctness before volume. Fourteen tickets (SEEN-114 to SEEN-123 and SEEN-135 to SEEN-138, 32 build points) put the tools in front of the model: rules from findings in the pre-commit hook, marketplace clients generated from the official OpenAPI contracts, property and mutation tests on the money core, the domain's own types (branded money and ids, one schema per boundary), a separate spec session that writes the RED from one worked example per criterion, a bounded review with a two-round cap, hermetic tests, parallel slices, affected-only CI, an LSP bake-off, a regression suite for the harness itself and a cap on harness work. They carry a priority (P0 before the first connector ticket, P1 before Sprint 0 closes, P2 when convenient) and the reason is in `docs/harness/workflow.md`: Sprint 0 delivered 63 review findings and 0.79 returns per ticket, and every finding a rule could have caught was paid for three times. The P0 tickets (SEEN-114, SEEN-115, SEEN-117, SEEN-118, SEEN-135) come before the first connector ticket, SEEN-011; SEEN-116 is P0 as well but waits for the fee schedules of SEEN-016, so it lands early in Sprint 1. That is why the ingest gate G0 is a Sprint 1 outcome in practice and is reviewed there.

Seller of record, groundwork now, build after day 120. The storefront (epic E11) lets a brand sell through Seen's own marketplace accounts when it cannot be the seller itself. Its human tickets sit in Sprints 2 to 4 because registrations, accounts and the legal shape have lead times of weeks (SEEN-124 to SEEN-127); the trade record learns connection ownership in Sprint 7 (SEEN-128); the listing, dropship, invoicing, statement and returns work is Sprint 8, after day 120, with one supplier live on Bol as the gate. It is sequenced this way because the MVP calendar to 29 January is full and the POC gate on 30 November is the priority; if a storefront pilot signs before then, Sprint 8 moves forward and a module ticket moves back, decided at the gate review.

One builder (Ruud, driving Claude Code, full days from 28 September once the Partao position is settled), one business partner on sales, design partners and the automotive network, no hires before the POC gate. Estimates are in points, where one point is roughly two hours of builder attention with Claude Code doing the typing, so a two-week sprint holds about 40 points of build plus the human tasks. Human tasks (registrations, contracts, sales conversations, approvals in the pilot inbox) carry a separate executor flag and are not counted against the build capacity. If a second builder joins in Sprint 3 or later, the modules from Sprint 4 onward are the parallel track; Sprints 0 to 2 are sequential by nature.

## Day-0 checklist (human, before or during Sprint 0)

Amazon SP-API developer registration for a public application with the Finance and Accounting, Product Listing, Pricing and Buyer Communication roles requested on day 0 because approval takes weeks; the Amazon Ads API application filed the same day. eBay developer account, production keys, OAuth consent flow and the Application Growth Check request. Bol partner platform API credentials for the friendly brand and the client-credentials flow. Kaufland and Otto seller API keys come from each customer at connection time. Shopify custom app on the friendly brand's store. Postmark inbound domain for the forwarded mailbox. Stripe account with SEPA and card, EU VAT settings. Domain and DNS; the GCP project in europe-west4 and the Supabase EU project wait for the go decision (SEEN-007). Legal: Partao contract read for IP and non-compete, a data processing agreement and Amazon data protection policy compliance statement for pilots, terms for the recovery share (billable on credits received only).

## Not in the MVP

The storefront beyond one supplier on Bol (Amazon, Kaufland and Otto under Seen's accounts follow the Bol pilot's go decision); the buy-resell variant of the storefront where Seen holds stock; consumer-facing branding of the storefront beyond a neutral store name. Zalando, Cdiscount, ManoMano and Autodoc Marketplace connectors (after day 120, in the order customers ask); the retailer-side product beyond a read-only view of open claims; purchase orders, refunds, account settings and delistings as agent actions (never in the MVP); repricing on Amazon until the Fair Pricing ceiling governor has run a month on eBay and Bol; a mobile app; SSO; anything a browser bot would need.

## Risks to the plan

The harness keeps eating the calendar: 24 harness tickets delivered before the first product ticket reached review, and 17 more sit in Sprint 0; SEEN-123 caps harness work at ten percent of a sprint from Sprint 1, the weekly report shows the share, and whatever Sprint 0 does not finish competes with product work under that cap rather than ahead of it. The storefront's legal shape or a registration (EPR, OSS, a marketplace account in Seen's entity) takes longer than the sprint it sits in: the human tickets start in Sprint 2 for that reason, and the Sprint 8 build waits for them rather than assuming them. The go-live decision slips past the first pilot approvals: the tunnel from the local environment carries the inbox meanwhile, and the DPA states where the data lives; if a pilot brand refuses that, go-live moves forward, not the pilot. Amazon role approval slower than Sprint 2: file on day 0, run Sprint 2 on eBay and Bol, fold Amazon in when approved. Bol compensation form with no structured fields: the case pack becomes copy-ready text and evidence files, still one click plus paste. Fewer than three friendly brands by 12 October: the partner's automotive network is the fallback list, and the audit runs on exported settlement files where an API key is late. Recovery pool on Amazon under 0.5%: the audit still finds Bol and eBay money, and the module conversion carries the plan; the day-120 pack reports the measured pool honestly.
